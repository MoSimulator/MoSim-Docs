While there is no "right" or "wrong" way to approach physics-based robots, we have found a way that tends to produce better results faster than other ways.

<img src="/img/mason/tuning-principles/overview.png" alt="Overview" width="50%"/>

## Simple

When looking at something, you should always focus on what the simplest way to approach it is. Avoid solutions that are obnoxiously jank or unnecessary.

<img src="/img/mason/tuning-principles/simple.png" alt="Simple" width="50%"/>

## Use Your Tools

The custom tooling we have created is designed with the explicit purpose of making things easier, faster, and simpler. Focus on learning to use and lean into it.

<img src="/img/mason/tuning-principles/tools.png" alt="Use your tools" width="50%"/>

## Try Things and Iterate

Don't try to do it all at once; slowly bring a robot up from nothing to functional. When fixing issues, make minor *intentional* tweaks with the goal of learning how the system behaves and how to make it behave better.

<img src="/img/mason/tuning-principles/iterate.png" alt="Iterate" width="50%"/>

## Practice

Every robot you work on makes the next one that much easier.

<img src="/img/mason/tuning-principles/practice.png" alt="Practice" width="50%"/>

## Robot Quality Matters

The complexity and quality of the team will affect how hard a robot is to add. Know this ahead of time and be ready. If a team had something fundamentally wrong IRL, that **will** likely carry over to the simulation. Better robots will typically work significantly faster than others.

<img src="/img/mason/tuning-principles/robot_quality.png" alt="Robot quality" width="50%"/>

## Look Beyond the Surface

Sometimes the issue isn't immediately obvious. A lot of things we have found have been hiding in plain sight, just below what you would normally look for.

<img src="/img/mason/tuning-principles/beyond_surface.png" alt="Look beyond the surface" width="50%"/>
